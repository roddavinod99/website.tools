## Why Generate QR Codes?

QR (Quick Response) codes are two-dimensional barcodes that store data — URLs, text, contact information, Wi-Fi credentials, payment details, and more — in a pattern scannable by any smartphone camera. They bridge the physical and digital worlds: a menu, poster, business card, or product label can carry a link, a coupon, or a Wi-Fi network setup in an instant. Because QR codes are universally supported by modern phone cameras with no app required, they are one of the most reliable ways to move someone from a printed or displayed surface to a digital destination.

## How QR Codes Work

A QR code encodes data in a grid of black and white modules, with three large position markers in the corners that cameras use to detect and orient the code. The same data can be encoded at different error-correction levels (L, M, Q, H): higher levels survive more damage or obstruction but produce denser codes. Numeric data packs most efficiently, followed by alphanumeric, then bytes — so a phone number encodes into a smaller code than an emoji-heavy text. Versions (1-40) control the grid size, which grows with the amount of data.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistakes are encoding more data than needed (which makes the code dense and hard to scan), using colors with poor contrast between modules and background, and placing codes too small or too close to other graphics. Always include enough quiet space (the white margin) around the code, keep the error-correction level at M or higher for printed material, and test on a real device in the lighting conditions where it will be scanned.
